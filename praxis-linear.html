I.
Broad Issue · Heading ↳ Arrangement (large-scale)
BROAD ISSUE that determines the organization for the memo.
Heading note Rule type and court treatment determine how much explanation each issue requires — and in what sequence. The preliminary structure of your headings may need adjustment once rule treatment becomes clear.
Canon · Invention

Inductive Reasoning

Every issue MUST have a RULE. In a multi-issue memo, this should be the BROAD rule(s) that govern the BROAD legal issue — statute, common law, etc. You may have to start broad (e.g., Contract) and funnel down to the elements at issue (e.g., offer, acceptance, consideration); or start with the statute and indicate interpretive common law. This rule section sets the roadmap for the forthcoming NARROW rules.

Construction Where does this rule sit in the legal hierarchy?
General hierarchy (constitution → statute → common law), but consider general rule and defenses / exceptions, etc., and how courts discuss rules.
Content What is the nature of this rule?
  • If statute: does plain language of the statute define the term, or do you need a definition from common law (operative)?
  • If common law: are there interpretations from case law (operative)? Do courts treat the term as ambiguous (regulative)? Do courts invoke purpose (optative)?
Rule Type How do I explain it given its structure?
  • Element-based: all elements at issue? Yes — locate rule language for each element and any sub-elements. No — acknowledge briefly. Interpreted term: research briefly.
  • Factors-based: per court, is any factor more important than others? Judicial weighing may signal policy.
  • Policy-based: may overlap above — distinction is whether purpose drives interpretation. Such rules require different treatment. Emphasize why the rule exists.

NARROW ISSUE #1 that must be analyzed / resolved to conclude on the Broad issue.
Canon · Invention

Inductive Reasoning

Every issue MUST have a RULE. Here, this is the NARROW rule(s) that govern Narrow Issue #1 (e.g., offer) — i.e., "padding."

Content What bucket does this element fall into?

The umbrella rule is established. You are now reporting what research reveals about this specific element — not reconstructing the framework.

  • Plain terms bucket (operative): state the kinds of facts or characteristics that satisfy the rule.
  • Ambiguous terms bucket (regulative): the analogical will show the range of interpretations courts have found valid.
  • Purpose-driven bucket (optative): the analogical will demonstrate the legislative rationale in action.
Canon · Invention

Analogical Reasoning

How can we help the court solve the problem by using precedent? This is stare decisis in action. We use precedent to illustrate how the rule works — construct the case discussion purposefully, mindful of the interpretations the court has already found valid, and consider how many cases we need to illustrate how the rule has / can work.

Goal
Use precedent to educate the court on the validity of rule application. Determine which case is most helpful — and why. Precedent signals stare decisis.
Approach
Fact-based (operative) rule — reliance on precedent may be minimal; one case may suffice.
Ambiguous / Policy (regulative · optative) — requires more explanation.
Structure
SynthesizedMultiple cases helpful to show the range of facts that can satisfy the rule.
UnsynthesizedSingle case for various reasons — e.g., facts on point; case marks a policy origin or the relevant starting point of law from which courts have since departed.
Canon · Invention

Deductive Reasoning

Use the precedent from analogical reasoning to map the legal reasoning from the precedent onto our client's facts — to help the court solve the problem.

  1. What did my analogical cases show that mattered to the courts — specific facts? interpreted terms? rule policy?
  2. Citing back to the analogical case(s), how do my client's facts explicitly address the same aspects the cases focused on?
  3. Does my application justify the conclusion by showing my facts satisfy what the analogical backing established?
Note Application is effective only if it addresses what your analogical reasoning established.
Counter Determine whether client facts raise an opposing argument or qualifier that requires a rebuttal; look to judicial opinions to identify counters.
Policy Will public policy strengthen the resolution — i.e., is the outcome not only legally correct, but does it further what the court identifies as important?
